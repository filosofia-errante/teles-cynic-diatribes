# Discourse I: On Seeming and Being
*(Περὶ τοῦ δοκεῖν καὶ τοῦ εἶναι)*

**Teles:** People say that seeming to be just is superior to actually being just. But surely seeming good is not superior to being good, is it?

**Interlocutor:** Certainly not.

**Teles:** Then do actors perform well on the stage because they *seem* to be good actors, or because they *actually are*?

**Interlocutor:** Because they are.

**Teles:** And do musicians play the cithara skillfully because they seem to be good players, or because they are?

**Interlocutor:** Because they are.

**Teles:** And in all other pursuits without exception, do people succeed because they seem capable, or because they actually are?

**Interlocutor:** Because they are.

**Teles:** And the condition through which men live well is superior to the one through which they do not. Therefore, being good is evidently superior to merely seeming good—for the just man is truly good, whereas the one who merely puts on the appearance of justice is not.

What about all the other things people consider goods? Would you prefer to truly possess them, or merely seem to possess them?

To take immediate examples: would you rather have real sight, or merely seem to see? To be healthy, or merely seem healthy? To be strong and vigorous, or merely seem so? To be well-provided and have true friends, or merely seem to have them?

Turning to the goods of the soul: would you rather possess genuine practical wisdom, or merely seem wise? To be untroubled by grief, or merely seem untroubled? To be confident, fearless, and courageous, or merely seem so? Why then, when it comes to justice alone, would you no longer choose being just over seeming just?

**Interlocutor:** Well, I would actually rather seem courageous than truly be so.

**Teles:** Isn't it the truly courageous man who is fearless and undisturbed, rather than the one who only wears the mask? Why on earth do you want to seem courageous?

**Interlocutor:** People will honor me.

**Teles:** Indeed! And because of that reputation, they will station you in the vanguard of the battle line; they will order you to face the enemy in single combat; they will rig the lottery so that your lot is drawn; and when you draw it, they will gloat over you with malicious glee, just as they did over Ajax.

What do you imagine you will suffer then, being a coward at heart while standing face-to-face with mortal danger?

And if you are captured as a prisoner of war, because you have the reputation of being a brave warrior, they will slap heavy iron chains and handcuffs on you. Not a soul will trust you; you will be locked up in close confinement; and if they interrogate you, you will take countless lashes. Even when you tell the absolute truth, they will never believe you—they will think you are mocking them because you are supposed to be tough and unyielding! So they will order the torturers to flog you, stretch you on the rack, and roast you over coals.

Look at what you earn for yourself merely by seeming brave and steadfast! You parade the public praise out front, while concealing all the gruesome consequences—just like dishonest politicians.
