# Discourse V: On Pleasure Not Being the Goal of Life
*(Περὶ τοῦ μὴ εἶναι τέλος ἡδονήν)*

### 1. The Tally of Life’s Stages

If a happy life must be judged by a surplus of pleasures over pains, says Crates, then no human being has ever lived happily[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span)[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span). If anyone takes the trouble to calculate all the stages throughout the entire span of life, he will find that troubles and grief are far more numerous[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span)[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).

First of all, half of our entire existence goes for nothing, passed in unconscious sleep[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span). 

Then consider the very first stage—childhood rearing—which is full of distress[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span)[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span). The infant is hungry, but the nurse rocks him to sleep; he is thirsty, but she douses him in a bath; he wants to sleep, but she shakes a rattle noisily in his face[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span)!

### 2. The Endless Taskmasters of Youth

If the boy escapes the nurse, he is handed over in turn to the tutor, the physical trainer, the reading master, the music instructor, and the painter[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span)[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span). 

As he grows older, yet more taskmasters are added: the arithmetic teacher, the geometry instructor, and the horse trainer[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span). He is dragged out of bed at dawn; there is not a single moment of leisure[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span)[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span)!

Now he becomes an adolescent (*ephebe*): he lives in fear of the youth magistrate, the wrestling master, the weapons instructor, and the director of the gymnasium[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span)[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span). By all of them he is beaten, constantly watched, and grabbed by the neck[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span)[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span).

He passes out of adolescence and reaches twenty years of age: yet still he lives in dread, anxiously watching both the gymnasiarch and the military general[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span)[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span). If soldiers are needed to camp out in the dirt, these youths must camp; if there is night guard and sleepless sentry duty, they must keep watch; if troops must board the naval warships, they are the ones forced to embark[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span).

### 3. The Burdens of Adulthood and Old Age

He reaches full manhood and the prime of life: he marches out on foreign military campaigns, travels on diplomatic embassies for the city, gets entangled in politics, serves as general, pays for dramatic choruses out of his own pocket, and presides over public games[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span)[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span)[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span)[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span). And what does he do through all this[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span)[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span)? He looks back and deems the life he lived as a boy to be the only truly happy time[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span)[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span)!

Then his prime fades and old age arrives: once again he is subjected to the nursing care of a helpless child, longing bitterly for his lost youth[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span)[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span):

> *'Youth was ever dear to me,*  
> *But old age is a burden heavier than Mount Aetna[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span)[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span)!'*

I simply fail to see how anyone could ever be said to have lived a happy life, if happiness must truly be measured by a surplus of pleasure[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span)[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span).
