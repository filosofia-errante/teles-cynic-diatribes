# Discourse VI: On Circumstances
*(Περὶ περιστάσεων)*

### 1. Playing the Role Assigned by Fortune

Fortune, like a playwright, creates roles of every kind: the shipwrecked sailor, the beggar, the exile, the famous man, the obscure outcast[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span)[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span). A good man must therefore play skillfully whatever part she assigns him[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span)[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span). 

- You have been shipwrecked? Play the shipwrecked man well[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span)!
- From prosperity you have fallen into poverty? Play the poor man well[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span)[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span):

> *'Equipped in small things, and equipped in great[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).'*

Be satisfied with whatever chance garment, plain diet, and humble service come your way, just like Laertes[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span)[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span):

> *'With an old servant woman who served him his food and drink[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span);'*

And he slept on the ground upon a pallet of leaves[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span)[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span):

> *'Lowly beds of fallen leaves were spread upon the earth[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span)[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span).'*

### 2. The Trap of Luxury

Such simple provisions are entirely sufficient for living calmly and in good health, unless one chooses to chase luxury[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span)[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span):

> *'For not in the belly's gorging does the good reside[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span)[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span),*  
> *Nor in the costly weave of a fine mantle, nor in the softness of a mattress[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span).'*

Euripides put it neatly[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span)[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span):

> *'Out of sheer luxury we hunt after schemes for endless varieties of food[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span)[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).'*

And not only foods, but scents for the nose and sounds for the ear[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span)[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span)! 

One must never strive for luxury when external conditions do not warrant it[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span)[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span). Act instead like sailors watching the winds and the surrounding conditions: if they are favorable, make use of them; if they turn unfavorable, stop and pull back[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span)[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span).

### 3. The Cynic’s Line of Retreat

On a military campaign, the soldier with a horse fights in the cavalry; the man with armor fights as a heavy hoplite; and the man with nothing fights as a light-armed skirmisher[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span)[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span)[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span)[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span). Just as in battle, when the enemy presses hard and launches a barrage of missiles, you retreat behind the shields because you are light-armed—so it is here in the battle of life[span_38](start_span)[span_38](end_span)[span_39](start_span)[span_39](end_span). 

When war, destitution, or sickness press hard upon you, make your strategic retreat[span_40](start_span)[span_40](end_span)[span_41](start_span)[span_41](end_span):

- Retreat to eating one meal a day[span_42](start_span)[span_42](end_span)[span_43](start_span)[span_43](end_span);
- Retreat to waiting on yourself without servants[span_44](start_span)[span_44](end_span)[span_45](start_span)[span_45](end_span);
- Retreat to the simple threadbare cloak[span_46](start_span)[span_46](end_span)[span_47](start_span)[span_47](end_span);
- And at the very last, retreat freely to Hades[span_48](start_span)[span_48](end_span)[span_49](start_span)[span_49](end_span)!
